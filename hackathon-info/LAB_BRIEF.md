## Lane 2: Clear it

**The question:** how do we turn four years into two?

- **The fast path:** land already zoned correctly, a ministerial approval and state-approved plans.
- **What makes it hard:**
  - 34 OC cities each have their own forms, fees and checklists.
  - City forms sometimes misread state law.
  - HCD issues letters correcting cities.
  - Projects also wait on plan check, utility and fire sign-offs, annual funding rounds, condo maps and tenant relocation.
- **Who is stuck:** a nonprofit project manager, a small builder, a homeowner adding an ADU, or a city permit tech.
- **Lab ideas:**
  1. pick the path for one address
  2. catch old law in live documents (ADU law moved from 65852.2 to 66310–66342)
  3. audit a city's checklist
  4. measure real timelines from HCD's Annual Progress Reports
  5. make HCD's enforcement letters findable
- **Real case:** a publicly listed small building or lot in Orange County.

**Where Euodos fits:** idea 1, picking the path for one address, plus the time model and the old-law check (statute numbers are current, with "formerly 65852.2" shown).

## Judging

Four equal areas, each scored 1–5. Ties go to Impact.

| Area | The question |
|---|---|
| Impact | Would a nonprofit or city use it next month? |
| Works | Does the live demo run on real data, with sources shown? |
| Scale | Is there a believable path to the next 100 projects? |
| Innovation | Is it a new way to see or solve the problem? |

What helps:
- one named user with a before and after
- a real public case
- sources and assumptions on screen
- "unknown" where it is unknown
- honesty about what is mocked

What does not place: slides without a demo, made-up numbers presented as facts, or real personal information.

## Rules we followed

- Public or provided data only, with no personal information; the persona and households are made up.
- Nothing files an application or contacts anyone, and the app says it is not legal advice.
- No invented statutes or deadlines: unknown clocks say "unknown", and assumptions are labelled.
- Public repo with an MIT license, a "Try it" section, and no keys (`.env.example` only).
